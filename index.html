Most teams use AI to draft an email or answer a question. Useful, but a long way from changing how the business runs. The real gains show up when AI is built into the workflow itself: reading the inputs, applying your rules, moving information between systems, finishing the routine work, and handing a person only the exceptions.

Those opportunities are specific to your company and rarely fit inside another piece of software. The people closest to the work don't have the time or the tools to rebuild it, and if you have engineers, they have a roadmap. So the manual work stays, and the lift everyone was promised never shows up. That's the gap we close.

03Who it's for

Companies with more operational work than engineering bandwidth.

A good fit
Fifty to five hundred employees, with a CEO or COO accountable for headcount and productivity.
Important workflows spread across email, spreadsheets, and software that doesn't connect.
Teams spending real time moving information, chasing people, and making the same decisions over and over.
Engineers, if you have them, focused on the product rather than the back office.
Leadership that wants measurable results this quarter, not an AI strategy for next year.
Not a fit
You want a chatbot, a workshop, or a proof of concept that nobody will own.
You need extra developers to work through a product backlog.
You want another off-the-shelf SaaS tool.
You're not prepared to change the underlying workflow once a better one is proven.
